# QAssist - User Personas

## Persona 1: Maya Chen - The Organized Individual Contributor

### Photo Description
*Mid-20s Asian woman with glasses, working on a laptop in a modern home office setup, natural lighting from window, wearing casual professional attire (hoodie), dual monitors visible in background, coffee mug on desk*

### Demographics
- **Age:** 27
- **Occupation:** QA Engineer at mid-size SaaS startup (40-person team)
- **Location:** Austin, Texas, USA
- **Education:** Bachelor's in Computer Science
- **Experience:** 3 years in QA (1 year manual testing, 2 years mixed manual/automation)
- **Work Model:** Hybrid (3 days remote, 2 days in office)

### Goals
- **Deliver high-quality test coverage** without spending hours on documentation overhead
- **Build a professional portfolio** of well-documented test work that she can take to future jobs
- **Reduce context switching** between multiple tools (Jira, Excel, Slack, folders) that fragment her workflow
- **Impress stakeholders** with polished, professional test documentation during sprint reviews

### Pain Points
- **Wastes 60-90 minutes daily** jumping between Jira (for team visibility), Excel (for detailed test cases), local folders (for screenshots), and text editors (for test data)
- **Duplicates work constantly**: Creates basic test cases in Jira for devs/PMs, then maintains detailed versions in Google Sheets for her own reference
- **Struggles with evidence management**: Screenshots accumulate in Downloads folder, hard to find specific evidence weeks later when bugs resurface
- **Fears losing work history**: When she left her previous company, lost access to all Jira test cases—had to rebuild knowledge from scratch at new job

### Tech Savviness
- **Profile:** Early Mainstream Adopter
- **Adoption Pattern:** Quick to try new productivity tools if they save time, but abandons tools with steep learning curves
- **Daily Tools:**
  - Jira (mandatory team tool)
  - Google Sheets (personal test case library)
  - Notion (personal notes and planning)
  - Slack, VS Code, Postman
  - GitHub (basic usage)
- **Device Ecosystem:** MacBook Pro (work), personal Windows laptop, iPhone

### Representative Quote
> "I spend more time organizing my test cases across three different tools than actually testing. When I left my last job, I lost two years of testing work because everything was locked in their Jira. Never again."

### QAssist Usage Pattern (Expected)
- **Primary Use Case:** Sync User Stories from Jira → Create detailed test cases in QAssist → Auto-organize screenshots by sprint
- **Upgrade Trigger:** Hits 50 test case limit on free tier after 2 weeks, values time savings enough to pay $12/month
- **Most Valued Features:** Jira bidirectional sync, evidence auto-organization, professional PDF exports
- **Frequency:** Uses 4-5 days/week, primarily during active testing phases of sprint

## Persona 2: Franco Martínez - The Juggling Freelancer

### Photo Description
*Early-30s Latino man with short beard, working at a café with laptop and notebook, wearing smart casual attire (button-up shirt), earbuds in, focused expression, multiple browser tabs visible on screen*

### Demographics
- **Age:** 34
- **Occupation:** Freelance QA Consultant (works with 3-5 clients simultaneously)
- **Location:** Barcelona, Spain (works with international clients across time zones)
- **Education:** Bachelor's in Information Systems + QA Certification (ISTQB)
- **Experience:** 8 years in QA (5 years in-house, 3 years freelance)
- **Work Model:** Fully remote, manages multiple client projects with overlapping deadlines

### Goals
- **Maintain separate workspaces** for each client without mixing test cases or evidence across projects
- **Own his professional portfolio**: Never lose access to test work when client contracts end
- **Deliver consistent quality** across all clients with reusable test case templates and patterns
- **Scale his consultancy** by working more efficiently—wants to take on 4th client without increasing hours

### Pain Points
- **Loses access to work immediately** when client contracts end—everything lives in their Jira instances
- **Context switching nightmare**: Managing 4 different Jira projects, each with different workflows, custom fields, and naming conventions
- **No centralized portfolio**: Can't easily show prospective clients examples of previous testing work—has to recreate sample test cases from memory
- **Inconsistent documentation quality** across clients due to time pressure—some projects get Excel test cases, others just Jira tickets

### Tech Savviness
- **Profile:** Early Adopter / Power User
- **Adoption Pattern:** Actively seeks tools that solve workflow pain, willing to invest time in setup if long-term payoff is clear
- **Daily Tools:**
  - Jira (4 different instances across clients)
  - TestRail (one client uses it)
  - Excel + Google Sheets (personal test libraries)
  - Notion (client meeting notes)
  - Figma (for annotating UI bugs)
  - Loom (recording bug reproductions)
  - Toggl (time tracking)
- **Device Ecosystem:** Custom-built Windows PC (home office), MacBook Air (travel), Android phone

### Representative Quote
> "Every time a contract ends, I lose months of testing work trapped in their Jira. I need to own my work—it's my professional reputation, not theirs. Plus, juggling four different Jira instances is driving me insane."

### QAssist Usage Pattern (Expected)
- **Primary Use Case:** Create separate projects for each client → Import their Jira stories → Maintain personal detailed test library → Export professional PDFs for client deliverables
- **Upgrade Trigger:** Immediately upgrades to Pro for unlimited projects (free tier's 1 project is non-starter)
- **Most Valued Features:** Unlimited projects, local data ownership, professional PDF exports with custom branding
- **Frequency:** Daily power user (6-7 days/week), uses across all active client projects

## Persona 3: Laura Kim - The Hands-On QA Lead

### Photo Description
*Late-30s Asian-American woman with shoulder-length hair, in a modern office setting, mid-conversation in video call on laptop, wearing business casual attire, whiteboard with sprint planning visible in background, confident and engaged expression*

### Demographics
- **Age:** 38
- **Occupation:** QA Team Lead at enterprise software company (manages team of 6 QA engineers)
- **Location:** Seattle, Washington, USA
- **Education:** Master's in Software Engineering
- **Experience:** 12 years in QA (8 years as engineer, 4 years in leadership)
- **Work Model:** Hybrid (leads team ceremonies in-office, testing work done remotely)

### Goals
- **Balance leadership and individual work**: Still does hands-on testing (40% of time) while managing team responsibilities
- **Maintain personal testing workspace** separate from team coordination chaos in Jira
- **Set quality standards for team**: Create test case templates and patterns that team members can adopt
- **Improve team productivity**: Identify and eliminate workflow inefficiencies that waste team's time

### Pain Points
- **Drowning in Jira noise**: Team's Jira project has 200+ tickets, making it hard to focus on her own testing work
- **No clean separation**: Personal test cases mixed with team tickets, hard to filter and find her individual contributions
- **Wants to share best practices** but lacks good mechanism—copying test case templates via Slack or Google Docs is clunky
- **Team struggles with same tools fragmentation** she experienced as IC, but no budget for expensive enterprise test management platforms ($10K+ annually)

### Tech Savviness
- **Profile:** Pragmatic Mainstream Adopter
- **Adoption Pattern:** Evaluates tools carefully, prioritizes team adoption ease over cutting-edge features, needs clear ROI
- **Daily Tools:**
  - Jira (team project management + personal testing)
  - Confluence (documentation)
  - Excel (test case templates)
  - Slack (team communication)
  - TestRail (evaluated but too expensive for budget)
  - Tableau (reporting)
  - GitHub (code reviews for automation)
- **Device Ecosystem:** Company-issued MacBook Pro, iPad for meetings, iPhone

### Representative Quote
> "I need a clean workspace for my own testing work, away from the team's Jira chaos. If this helps me work faster, I want my team to use it too—but I can't afford $500/user/year for TestRail on our budget."

### QAssist Usage Pattern (Expected)
- **Primary Use Case (Phase 1 - Individual):** Personal workspace for her testing work → Sync only her assigned Jira stories → Create and refine test case templates
- **Future Use Case (Phase 2 - Team Plan):** Share test case templates with team → Centralized evidence repository → Track team productivity improvements
- **Upgrade Trigger:** Upgrades to Pro immediately for personal use, advocates for Team Plan once she sees individual productivity gains
- **Most Valued Features (Individual):** Personal workspace clarity, template reusability, offline mode for focused work
- **Most Valued Features (Team - Future):** Shared templates, team usage analytics, centralized evidence library
- **Frequency:** 3-4 days/week (testing phases), lighter usage during planning/coordination weeks

## Insights & Implications for MVP

### Design Priorities
1. **Sub-5 minute onboarding** is critical—all three personas have limited tolerance for setup complexity
2. **Jira sync must be bulletproof**—it's the hook that gets users to try the product
3. **Local data ownership messaging** resonates strongly with Franco (and Maya post-job-change trauma)

### Feature Priorities
1. **Maya needs:** Evidence auto-organization, professional PDF exports, simple UI
2. **Franco needs:** Unlimited projects, portable data, custom branding on exports
3. **Laura needs:** Personal workspace clarity, template reusability, future team sharing

### Pricing Strategy Validation
- **Maya:** Will try free tier, converts when hitting limits (~2-3 weeks) if she sees time savings
- **Franco:** Bypasses free tier entirely—needs unlimited projects from day 1 ($12/month is business expense)
- **Laura:** Uses free/Pro individually first, becomes team plan advocate if successful (future $8/user/month opportunity)

### Marketing Channels
- **Maya:** Reddit (r/QualityAssurance), LinkedIn posts, YouTube tutorials
- **Franco:** Freelancer communities, LinkedIn direct outreach, word-of-mouth in consultant networks
- **Laura:** QA leadership forums, conference talks (post-validation), case studies showing team productivity gains

### Risk Mitigation
- **If Maya churns:** Onboarding too complex or time savings not immediately visible
- **If Franco doesn't upgrade:** Free tier 1-project limit wasn't restrictive enough, or lack of trust in data portability
- **If Laura doesn't advocate for team:** Individual benefits don't translate to clear team-level ROI story
